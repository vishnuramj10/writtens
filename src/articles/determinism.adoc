I had always been a person who didn't have much trouble falling asleep. Even as a little child with a comic book and a torch under the covers, staying up past ten was a rarity. But we all grow up, and for us, social media wormed its way into our life like a vapid pet snake. It's hard now to fall asleep without a youtube video playing in the background. Anything, really, to avoid my thoughts alone in the dark.

It is sometimes a curse to be a curious child who cannot find answers. Mostly from laziness, but let's not talk about that. Eventually your questions lead you right to the doors of an existential crisis. You open them yourself. Existentialism, I think, is the shining meeting point of all our scientific knowledge. The sangam of biology, chemistry and physics. The ultimate question of why, and how. When you lie in the dark as a sixteen year old and ask yourself what it means to be an alive thing in a cold universe, it kind of fucks with any of our heads, doesn't it? But anyway, subjective meaning is a built thing. Individually, painstakingly. The closest scientific answer that we have to the question why, I think, is determinism.

Determinism is the view that things can only happen within our universe in one possible way. Everything, hence, is essentially predetermined; not by a god or some great entity in the sky but by the very physical laws that bind our existence. That atoms and their parts can only interact with each other in a specific way bound by the laws of physics and its forces.

Determinism then is when we ask why things happen, all the answer we get or can ever get is the universe shrugging its nebulous shoulders. This is the way it is. We're all made of atoms. Every living thing, only a reaction. The atoms that make up the neurons and neurotransmitters of our brains function under code, not only evolutionary but also the immediate physical that influences the actions we take.

So, if I choose to wear my neon green pants today because they make me happy, it's not a choice at all but the culmination of the millions of interactions that the atoms of my brain have had throughout my life. It could not be any other way. The same logic would also apply to crime, I suppose. If one commits murder because their brain particles pirouetted a certain way, is it really their fault? I will leave that one there for us all to chew on uncomfortably.

My favourite part about this though is that it makes us no different than microscopic unicellular things. We're all just an organism, floating through time and space, reacting to any stimuli just as directed by our chemical and physical interactions with our surroundings. Out of the dust, a little puppet dance, then back under.

There is only so much we can know or do in our lifetime. We don't live nearly long enough. But this is at least a reminder to loosen our grip once in a while. To take a deep breath and look at the trees and the sky; and know that we are just the universe, experiencing itself.
