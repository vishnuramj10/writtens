I thought maybe if I start writing, it'll make me feel better.

While it's easier to say, "I am starting my PhD," as I start the journey in two weeks, the imposter syndrome is kicking in really hard. The application process was quite easier. My professor selected me through a referral, but I am hoping he thinks I can do it and that I am good enough. The fear is creeping onto me as the days go by. I am fine, and I outright enjoyed my summer, but the syndrome has been kicking in strong.

The only thing I am going forward with is that I still want to do it.

I don't have any prior research experience. My advisor selected me through a referral, and I'd like to believe it was because they saw my passion and willingness to learn. But now I keep wondering: will that passion be enough to carry me through the next few years?

What if I don't come up with good research ideas?

What if I'm not capable of doing research?

What if I can't make it through the entire journey?

What if I'm not as smart as I thought I was?

The only thing is, I do not regret choosing it until now.

It was certainly not an easy decision to make. My friends did tell me that I have to prepare for my life being stagnant. Everyone else will move up, get married, get a high-paying job, but I will be on a journey, most likely stuck on a problem. Although I did think it through then, all of these thoughts are climbing onto me now.

Why did I choose this path? I could have continued my job, lived in the same apartment as my friends, gotten married like others are, and had a settled life. It's something my mom keeps telling me.

At the same time, I do think—I cannot do this job like others. I started liking studying during my master's, and even now, when I sit down to do my research assignment, even though it's not a topic I am comfortable with, I love working on it.

Why am I doing this?

Is it because I do not want to be like others?

Is it because I cannot do a job?

Is it because I like studying a lot?

I might have liked it during my master's, but what if I don't have the same passion now?

While all these thoughts pass across my mind, I am still excited for the new journey, and I hope my passion and interest in deep diving into a subject is something which will keep me going.
